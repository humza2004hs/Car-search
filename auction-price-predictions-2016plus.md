## How these prices were worked out

1. **Starting point.** For each car I took the private-party value (what a regular person would pay another person) from `data/market/`.
2. **Matched to the real sales.** Two cars actually sold at this auction: B-024 Ford Edge for $5,800 (about 41% of its private-party value) and B-085 Santa Fe for $3,500 (about 37%). So the base range is **36% to 46% of private-party value**. Both real prices land inside their predicted ranges.
3. **Adjustments**, multiplied together:
   - Title Absent: about 10% lower (dealers wait weeks for paperwork).
   - Carfax failures: salvage, rebuilt, total loss or Not Actual Mileage cars drop to about 45–55% of a clean car's price. Ex-rental cars drop about 10%. A clean Carfax with good records adds a little.
   - Ex-police (A-010, A-076): about 25% lower, because the engines idle for long hours, so the miles shown understate the wear.
   - 180,000+ miles: about 10% lower on top of the mileage already priced into the private-party value.
   - Repair risk (from `data/judged.json`): high-risk models about 8% lower; low-risk, in-demand models about 5% higher.
4. Rounded to the nearest $100.

**Things to know:**
- Only 8 of these cars had a Carfax run (A-032, A-035, A-040, B-002, A-084, B-085, C-021, C-071). "Not checked" means the car's history is unknown, not that it is clean.
- B-004 (Chevy Express cargo van) is a work van with no back seats. It's priced anyway, but the repo had no market data for it, so I estimated its value at about $13,000–16,000 private party. Treat that range as a rough guess.
- C-031 shows 27,833 miles, which is probably a typo for 127,833, so its range is extra wide.
- These predictions only go as far as the data. A car with obvious damage, warning lights or a strong buyer in the room can sell outside its range.
